# Executive Summary: UAC Care Pipeline Performance Analysis

**Prepared for Government Stakeholders**

**Analysis Period:** January 2023 – December 2025

---

## Situation Overview

The Unaccompanied Alien Children (UAC) Program operates a multi-stage care pipeline:

```
Apprehension & CBP Custody → Transfer to HHS Care → Discharge to Sponsor Placement
```

This analysis evaluates how effectively children moved through this pipeline over a 35-month period, measuring transition efficiency, discharge performance, backlog accumulation, and outcome stability.

**The defining finding:** The system underwent a dramatic transformation in January 2025, transitioning from a high-volume operation processing 100-300+ children daily to a near-dormant state processing fewer than 20 children daily.

---

## Key Performance Metrics

| Metric | Definition | High-Volume Period (2023-2024) | Low-Volume Period (2025) |
|--------|------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Transfer Efficiency Ratio** | Transfers ÷ CBP Custody | Typically 0.1 - 0.5 (functional) | Volatile due to small numbers |
| **Discharge Effectiveness** | Discharges ÷ HHS Care | 0.01 - 0.05 (1-5% daily turnover) | Higher ratios due to smaller population |
| **Pipeline Throughput** | 7-day Discharges ÷ 7-day Apprehensions | Frequently below 1.0 (intake exceeded exits) | Volatile; often above 1.0 (exits exceeded intake) |
| **Backlog Accumulation Rate** | 7-day avg of (Apprehensions - Discharges) | Positive (net accumulation) | Negative (net reduction) |
| **Outcome Stability Score** | 1 - (CV of discharges) over 14 days | Moderate to high stability | Low stability (small-number volatility) |

**Note:** Formulas for Backlog Accumulation Rate and Outcome Stability Score are implementation-defined. The requirements described these concepts but did not specify exact formulas.

---

## Critical Findings

### 1. System Volume Contracted Dramatically

| Indicator | Peak (2023-2024) | Current (Dec 2025) | Change |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|--------|
| HHS Care Population | ~11,000+ | ~2,400 | ~78% reduction |
| Daily Apprehensions | 100-300+ | <20 | ~90%+ reduction |
| Daily Discharges | 100-400+ | Single digits to ~20 | ~90%+ reduction |
| CBP Custody | Up to 531 | Single digits | ~95%+ reduction |

The contraction began abruptly in January 2025 and transformed the operational context for all metrics.

### 2. Transfer Operations Remained Functional

- Transfer Efficiency Ratios indicated adequate CBP → HHS transition performance throughout the study period
- No significant CBP custody accumulation occurred
- The transfer mechanism operated as designed even during high-volume periods

### 3. Discharge Capacity Was Insufficient During High-Volume Period

- Pipeline Throughput frequently fell below 1.0 during 2023-2024
- Weekly apprehensions consistently exceeded weekly discharges
- This imbalance drove backlog accumulation and extended time-in-system

### 4. Backlog Accumulated During High-Volume Period

- HHS care population grew from ~6,500 to 11,000+ during 2023
- The accumulated population represented delayed reunifications
- Backlog was reduced only when intake collapsed in 2025, not through increased discharge capacity

### 5. Outcome Stability Degraded Due to Small-Number Volatility

- Stability scores were acceptable during high-volume periods
- Scores became unreliable in 2025 due to single-digit daily discharges
- This reflects measurement limitation, not true operational instability

### 6. Weekend and Holiday Gaps Created Predictable Inefficiencies

- Transfer and discharge activity consistently lower on weekends
- Holiday periods showed extended reporting gaps
- These gaps extended average time-in-system and created post-gap workload spikes

---

## KPIs Requiring Attention

| KPI | Status | Issue |
|-----|--------|-------|
| **Pipeline Throughput** | **ATTENTION REQUIRED** | Structural imbalance during high-volume period (throughput < 1.0) indicates discharge capacity was insufficient |
| **Backlog Accumulation Rate** | **MONITOR** | While currently negative (reduction), backlog accumulated significantly during 2023-2024; system should be prepared for potential future accumulation |
| **Outcome Stability Score** | **INTERPRET WITH CAUTION** | 2025 scores are unreliable due to small-number volatility; do not interpret low scores as evidence of operational problems |
| **Transfer Efficiency Ratio** | **ACCEPTABLE** | No significant concerns; consider establishing formal targets |
| **Discharge Effectiveness** | **INTERPRET CONTEXTUALLY** | Improved ratios in 2025 reflect smaller population, not process improvements |

---

## Recommended Actions

### 1. Maintain Surge Capacity Planning
**Priority: High**

The system operated at high volume for extended periods and may face volume increases in the future. Prepare documented surge capacity protocols including:
- Staffing contingency plans
- Facility capacity reserves
- Transfer and discharge acceleration procedures
- Clear escalation thresholds based on intake levels

### 2. Reduce Weekend and Holiday Processing Gaps
**Priority: Medium**

Implement reduced-but-continuous processing schedules for weekends and holidays:
- Weekend transfer processing (even at reduced capacity)
- Weekend discharge processing
- Holiday coverage plans

**Expected impact:** Reduced average time-in-system and smoother workload distribution.

### 3. Establish Formal Transfer Efficiency Targets
**Priority: Medium**

Implement explicit targets with monitoring protocols:
- Target Transfer Efficiency Ratio: maintain above 0.3 on rolling average (suggested threshold)
- Daily monitoring with weekly summary reporting
- Escalation procedures when targets are missed for consecutive days

### 4. Enhance Data Granularity for Improved Analysis
**Priority: Medium**

Current aggregate data limits analytical insight. Consider collecting:
- Individual case duration (time from apprehension to discharge)
- Sponsor relationship category
- Geographic destination
- Reason codes for extended stays

This would enable targeted process improvement efforts.

### 5. Provide Interpretation Guidance for Low-Volume Metrics
**Priority: Low (Immediate Implementation)**

When presenting KPIs for the low-volume period (2025):
- Clearly note that metrics may be affected by small-number volatility
- Consider using longer aggregation windows (14-day or monthly) for more stable measures
- Avoid over-interpreting short-term fluctuations

---

## Data Limitations

This analysis is based on aggregate daily counts and has important limitations:

1. **No individual-level tracking** — Cannot determine how long specific children spent in the system or identify factors associated with extended stays

2. **No geographic breakdown** — Cannot identify location-specific patterns or bottlenecks

3. **No reason codes** — Cannot determine why transfers or discharges were delayed in specific cases

4. **Non-consecutive reporting** — Weekends and holidays sometimes have reporting gaps

5. **Cannot establish causation** — Analysis identifies patterns but cannot determine causal relationships

6. **Small-number effects** — Metrics become unreliable when daily volumes are very low (single digits)

---

## Bottom Line

**What is happening in the pipeline:** The UAC care system has transformed from a high-volume operation to a near-dormant state. This transformation resolved accumulated backlog but was driven by external factors reducing intake, not by improved discharge capacity.

**Where bottlenecks occurred:** During 2023-2024, the discharge stage was a structural bottleneck. Apprehensions consistently exceeded discharges, leading to population growth and delayed reunifications.

**Outcome trajectory:** Placement outcomes (as measured by throughput and discharge metrics) showed structural imbalance during high-volume periods. Current metrics appear improved but this reflects reduced volume rather than enhanced capacity.

**KPIs requiring attention:** Pipeline Throughput (structural imbalance during high-volume period) and Outcome Stability Score (measurement unreliability during low-volume period).

**Actions supported by this analysis:**
1. Maintain surge capacity for potential volume increases
2. Reduce weekend/holiday processing gaps
3. Establish formal KPI targets
4. Enhance data granularity for improved analysis

The system should be prepared for potential future volume increases. The current low-volume state has resolved the backlog but should not be assumed permanent.

### Technical Note on KPI Formulas

**Formulas explicitly defined in requirements:**
- Transfer Efficiency Ratio = Transfers ÷ CBP Custody
- Discharge Effectiveness = Discharges ÷ HHS Care
- Pipeline Throughput Rate = Total Exits ÷ Total Entries (operationalized as 7-day rolling Discharges ÷ 7-day rolling Apprehensions)

**Formulas defined by this implementation (requirements provided concept only):**
- Backlog Accumulation Rate = 7-day rolling mean of (Apprehensions - Discharges)
- Outcome Stability Score = 1 - min(rolling_std ÷ rolling_mean, 1), calculated over 14-day window for discharges

All formulas are documented in the accompanying research paper.
